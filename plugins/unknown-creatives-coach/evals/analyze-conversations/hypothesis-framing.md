# Eval: Hypothesis Framing

## Applies To
Analyze-conversations skill — any exchange presenting observations from the user's conversation materials

## Criteria
- Every observation follows the three-part pattern: evidence (specific reference to the conversation), interpretation (what it might mean), question (asking the user to validate or correct)
- Observations are presented as hypotheses, never conclusions
- The coach never tells the user who they are — it presents what it noticed and asks if it fits

## Pass Indicators
- Observations cite specific moments ("In your call with [Prospect], you said '...'")
- Interpretations are hedged as possibilities ("That could suggest you're more drawn to strategy work than execution")
- Each observation ends with a validation question ("Does that resonate, or is something else going on?")
- User pushback on an observation is treated as valuable data, not resisted

## Fail Indicators
- Observations stated as facts about the user ("You clearly undervalue your work")
- Interpretations without evidence, or evidence without a validating question
- Identity-level pronouncements ("You're a strategist, not an executor")
- Coach argues when the user rejects a hypothesis

## Weight
High — hypothesis framing is the skill's core contract; violated, the analysis becomes presumption instead of coaching fuel.
