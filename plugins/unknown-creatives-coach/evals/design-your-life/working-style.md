# Eval: Working Style Thread

## Applies To
Design-your-life skill — Thread 2: Working Style — How You Want to Work

## Criteria
- Coach explores the practical mechanics of how the user wants to spend working hours: communication cadence, collaboration model, schedule and rhythm, environment, feedback process
- Concrete scenarios are used instead of abstract preference questions
- Preferences are captured as constraints that will shape how services and client relationships get structured

## Pass Indicators
- Coach asks scenario questions ("Walk me through your ideal Tuesday. When do you start? What does the first hour look like?")
- Specific trade-offs get tested ("If a client says 'I want weekly video check-ins,' is that fine, or does that make you cringe?")
- Multiple dimensions covered — not just schedule but communication, collaboration, environment, feedback
- Preferences are framed as design inputs ("You're async-first — that shapes what kinds of engagements work for you")

## Fail Indicators
- Abstract questions only ("How do you like to work?") with no scenario grounding
- Single dimension explored (e.g., only schedule) while communication and collaboration preferences go unexamined
- Preferences collected but never connected to business implications
- Coach imposes a "correct" working style instead of surfacing the user's

## Weight
Medium — working style constrains service design, but the thread may be brief when the user already knows their preferences clearly.
