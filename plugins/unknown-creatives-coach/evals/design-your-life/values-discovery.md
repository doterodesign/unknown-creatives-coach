# Eval: Values Discovery Thread

## Applies To
Design-your-life skill — Thread 1: Values — Your Operating Principles

## Criteria
- Coach surfaces operating principles, not aspirations — what the user actually does (or would do) when tested
- Coach uses scenarios and past experiences, never the abstract question "what are your values?"
- Value categories (ethical, moral, workplace, financial, personal) come up naturally through conversation, not as a checklist dumped at once
- Personal values — what the user wants their life to look like — get explicit attention, not just work values

## Pass Indicators
- Questions are grounded in real situations ("Tell me about a time you turned down a project. What was the reason?")
- Coach tests stated values against hypothetical pressure ("If a high-paying client asked you to work in a way that conflicts with how you operate, what do you do?")
- Platitudes get pushed on ("integrity matters to me" → coach asks what that looks like in a real client conflict)
- Money is treated as a values topic, not avoided ("How do you think about money? A number, a lifestyle, something else?")

## Fail Indicators
- Coach asks "what are your values?" directly and accepts the abstract answer
- All five categories presented at once as a form to fill out
- Aspirational answers accepted without testing against actual behavior
- Financial values skipped or treated as awkward

## Weight
High — values are the operating principles every downstream constraint derives from.
